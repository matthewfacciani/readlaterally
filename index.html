The idea

Three tiers. Most claims never reach the third.

0

~1 second

Already fact-checked?

We search a database of published fact-checks first. If professionals already looked at this, you see their findings immediately. Half of claims can end right here.

1

~10 seconds

The one question that catches the most

If nothing's on file, we skip the checklist and ask the single most useful thing: do you recognize who's behind this, and would they benefit from you believing it? For a lot of claims, that's enough.

The full lateral read

When a claim actually warrants it, dig deeper: who's behind it, where it originated, who else is covering it, with one-click searches and coaching at each step. This is the real skill, on demand rather than by default.

Case file One claim, all three tiers

"New study says this one common food doubles your risk of heart disease."

Tier 0

The scan finds a related fact-check: one outlet rated a near-identical headline Missing Context. That's already a signal, in about a second, that the framing is contested.

Tier 1

Who's behind it? The claim traces to a supplement retailer's blog, and they'd benefit from you distrusting the food. That's the reflex firing.

Tier 2

Dig deeper and the "study" turns out to be a conference abstract, not a peer-reviewed paper. "Doubles your risk" means going from 2% to 4%. Two other major outlets never covered it at all.

The tool never said true or false. You now know exactly why you'd hold this one loosely, and you got there in under two minutes.

The method, in a word: SCAN

  • S Search if it's already been checked
  • C Consider the source, and who benefits
  • A Ask who else is saying it
  • N Navigate elsewhere if you're still unsure

Why it works this way

It builds your judgment. It doesn't replace it.

No verdict, ever

The tool never tells you a claim is true or false. It shows you how to find out, because a credibility score you don't understand is just another thing to take on faith.

Lateral, not vertical

Fact-checkers don't judge a source by studying the source. They leave it and see what the rest of the web says. That single habit is the whole method.

Fast on purpose

The point of checking quickly is knowing what to ignore, so your attention goes to things that deserve it. A quick exit is a win, not a failure.

Nothing stored

What you check isn't logged or sold. The analysis happens, and then it's gone. No accounts, no tracking.
